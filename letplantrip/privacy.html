letplantrip · Privacy Policy

Last updated: 19 November 2026

What data the app handles

letplantrip is a trip planner. Everything it handles stays on your iPhone, in the app's local database:

  • Trips: name, dates or length, first and last day, the cities of the way out and back, and a cover photo chosen among the photos that ship with the app.
  • Stops and journeys: each stop's name, nights, note and position on the map; between stops, the means of transport, the local times as on your ticket, a flight or train number, nights on the road and a note.
  • Places and days: each place's name, category, note, link, postal address, position on the map and the days it is planned on; the things to do on each day, with their time and comment.
  • The Account screen: an optional name, which stays on this iPhone and is not included in backups, and your settings for appearance, lock and reminders.
  • Backups you make yourself: files holding all your trips, encrypted with your recovery phrase.
  • The recovery phrase, made at random by the app, and a marker of this iPhone (the date and time the app first ran on it), both in this iPhone's keychain. The marker appears in the names of backup files, so the app can tell its own copies from those of your other iPhones.

There is no account, no sign-in, and the developer has no server: the app sends nothing to the developer. It has no advertising and no analytics, embeds no third-party code, and is not directed at children; whatever your age, nothing is collected by the developer.

How this data is obtained

  • Your own input: everything you type in the app.
  • A place shared from another app, such as Google Maps: the share sheet of letplantrip receives its link, and reads the place's name and position from the address that link leads to (see the Google section below).
  • Positions on the map: found through Apple Maps from a stop's name or a place's address (see below), or placed by you on the map.
  • The example trip: added by the app only when you ask for it, with made-up content.

What the data is used for

Everything serves the app's visible features and nothing else: building your trip stop by stop and day by day, the map of your stops and places, ideas of places near the stop where you sleep, backups and their reminders, and the lock of the app. There is no profiling, no advertising, and no sharing for any other purpose.

Who else sees it

No third party receives your trips. The app makes a few connections, each for one feature, and adds no account or identifier of its own to them:

  • Apple Maps tiles: map backgrounds are loaded from Apple while the Map tab is on screen.
  • Apple Maps search: to place your stops and places on the map, the app sends Apple a stop's name, or a place's postal address (its name when it has neither position nor address), with the area where to look, and nothing else; once for each name or address, when the Map tab opens. The Maps search of the Map tab sends Apple what you type in it, as you type, with the region of your trip as a hint.
  • Google, from the share sheet only: when you share a Google Maps link, the share sheet sends that link, and only that link, to Google's own addresses, one step at a time, to learn where it leads. It keeps no cookie and reads no page. An address outside Google's official list of domains is never followed.
  • iCloud Drive, only if you turn on “Copy to iCloud Drive”: each backup, already encrypted with your recovery phrase, is copied to the app's folder in your iCloud Drive, keeping the 10 most recent of this iPhone. Apple stores these files; without your phrase, their content cannot be read.
  • Your iPhone backup (iCloud or computer): as for any app, the app's database and the backups kept in it are included in the device backup, under the protections Apple applies to it. The recovery phrase never moves to another device; see below.
  • Files and the share sheet: only when you share a backup, or pick a file to restore, through the picker or share sheet you control.

How long it is kept, and how to delete

Data stays on your iPhone until you delete it. You can delete a trip, a stop, a place or a backup in the app. Deleting the app removes its database and the backups kept in it from the iPhone. Copies in iCloud Drive stay until you delete them: in the app, with “Delete everywhere” on a backup, or by turning off “Copy to iCloud Drive” and choosing “Delete the copies”; or in the Files app.

The recovery phrase is kept in this iPhone's keychain, behind Face ID or your passcode, and never moves to another device. Unlike the rest, iOS keeps it after the app is deleted: reinstalled on the same iPhone, the app finds it again and opens your earlier backups. If it is lost, encrypted backups cannot be opened by anyone, the developer included.

Your permissions and how to withdraw them

  • Face ID: asked when you turn on the lock of the app, and when you show or check your recovery phrase. Withdraw it in Settings → Face ID & Passcode → Other Apps; the app then asks for your passcode instead.
  • Notifications: asked only when you first choose how often to be reminded of backups. Reminders are scheduled on the iPhone and never mention a trip. Withdraw them in Settings → Notifications → letplantrip.
  • iCloud Drive: off until you turn on “Copy to iCloud Drive” in Account; turn it off there at any time.

The app asks for nothing else: no location, no camera, no photo library, no contacts, no microphone. It keeps no log of your trips: on an error, it leaves a short technical message in the iPhone's own log, with an error description or code, never the content of your trips.
