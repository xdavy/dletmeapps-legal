LetMeRemind · Privacy Policy

Last updated: 30 October 2026

The data the app handles

LetMeRemind is an iPhone app, for iOS 26 or later, that keeps track of what comes back: bills, credits, treatments, maintenance, contracts, documents. It has no account and no server: everything it handles stays on your iPhone.

  • Your items and reminders: names, categories, dates and recurrences, amounts, notes, a link if you add one, and the history of each due date: paid, done or taken, when, and the amount actually paid.
  • Your treatments: the names of medicines, written by you as free text, with no dosage. The app gives no medical advice.
  • Your documents: PDFs and photos, up to 20 MB each, with their title.
  • Your settings, such as the default time of notifications and the display choices.
  • The lock of the app, if you turn it on: a salted hash of your 6-digit code, the waiting time after wrong codes, and a fingerprint, provided by iOS, of the state of the faces enrolled in Face ID. They are kept in the iPhone’s keychain, for this iPhone only. The app never sees your face or any biometric data.

The app uses no location, contacts, calendar, Health data, microphone, or advertising identifier.

Everything is stored in the space reserved for the app on your iPhone and protected by iOS encryption: the database can be read once the iPhone has been unlocked after it starts, your documents only while the iPhone is unlocked. The app adds no encryption of its own to the database. In the app switcher, the screen of the app is hidden.

How the data gets into the app

  • You type it. Nothing is filled in from another source.
  • The document scanner of iOS uses the camera, only when you ask for it; a scan becomes a PDF.
  • The photo picker of iOS gives the app only the photo you choose; the app has no access to your photo library. The photo is reduced to 2,560 pixels on its longer side and saved as a JPEG.
  • The Files app: you can add a PDF or an image from it, or import a LetMeRemind backup.

The app collects nothing by itself: no analytics, no tracking, no identifier. It collects no data from anyone, children included.

What the data is used for

  • Showing your due dates, your history, and your totals, on your iPhone.
  • Notifications, scheduled on your iPhone up to 90 days ahead, without going through the internet. An ordinary notification shows the name of the reminder, its due date, the number of medicines, and the amount. A “Private” reminder only shows “You have a reminder”; treatments and health reminders are private from the moment they are created. The intakes of a treatment, the follow-ups, and the return of a snoozed reminder are Time Sensitive notifications, which can come through a Focus.
  • Alarms (AlarmKit), if you turn them on for a treatment: the iPhone rings or vibrates at the time of an intake, even in silent mode, in place of the notification of that intake. The alarm screen is drawn by iOS: it shows the name of the treatment, or “You have a reminder” for a private one, and a “Taken” button that opens the app. The app shows no Live Activity of its own.
  • Background refresh: iOS wakes the app when it decides, at the earliest four hours after you leave it, then at most once every four hours, to reschedule your notifications and alarms.
  • Maintenance, each time the app opens or comes back to the foreground: it erases what was deleted more than 30 days ago, removes the files of documents that no longer exist, and closes the reminders that have ended.
  • Export and import: you can export your data to a .lmrbackup file (LMRB2 format), encrypted with AES-256-GCM by a key derived from your password (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 600,000 rounds). The password, 8 characters or more, is never stored: without it, the file can’t be read, and the developer can’t recover it. Without the password, only the date of the export, the version of the app, and the settings of the encryption can be read. Your documents are included unless you turn off “Include documents,” and so are the items deleted less than 30 days ago, without the file of a deleted document.
  • Error logs: when an error occurs, the app writes it to the iPhone’s system log. iOS hides its details by default, which may quote a stored value; the app never sends this log.

No advertising, no profiling, no sale of data.

Who else sees the data

No one: the developer has no server and receives none of your data. The app contains no third-party code.

Some Apple services receive a copy, when you use them:

  • iPhone backup: if iCloud Backup or the computer backup is on, iOS copies the data of the app into it, documents included. The app syncs nothing with iCloud: your data is not shared between your devices.
  • Lock Screen: iOS shows the notifications and alarms as described above.
  • Files and iCloud Drive: an export goes where you choose.
  • Sharing: the document viewer of iOS lets you share a document, if you do it yourself.
  • Safari: a link you added to a reminder opens there when you tap it; the website then sees your visit.
  • Crash reports and TestFlight: if you agreed, in iPhone Settings, to share analytics with app developers, Apple may send the developer anonymous crash reports. During the beta, the screenshots and comments you send through TestFlight reach the developer through Apple.

How long the data is kept, and how to delete it

Your data stays on your iPhone until you delete it.

  • Deleting an item or a reminder removes it from the app; it is kept 30 more days, so that an import does not bring it back, then erased the next time the app opens.
  • Deleting a document from its page erases it at once, with its file. The documents of a deleted item follow the 30-day rule.
  • The notifications already shown for a due date you handled are removed.
  • The app has no “erase all” command: deleting the app erases its data from your iPhone. If the lock was on, iOS keeps its three items in the keychain until a new installation of LetMeRemind erases them when it first starts.
  • Backups of the iPhone already made and exported files are not touched: you delete them in iCloud, on your computer, or in Files.

Your permissions, and how to withdraw them

Each permission can be withdrawn in iPhone Settings, on the LetMeRemind page. Withdrawing one erases none of your data.

  • Notifications, asked during the presentation of the app. Turned off, the app schedules no notification and clears its badge; its Settings say so, with a button to iPhone Settings, and “Remind me in 30 minutes” is no longer offered. In the notification settings, you can also turn off Time Sensitive notifications alone.
  • Alarms, asked the first time you turn on the alarm of a treatment. Refused, the switch goes back off; withdrawn later, no alarm rings any more, and the intakes are notified again.
  • Face ID, asked when you turn on the lock. Turned off for LetMeRemind, the app asks for its 6-digit code instead; it never asks for the iPhone passcode.
  • Camera, asked by iOS the first time you scan. Turned off, the scanner can no longer use the camera; you can still add a PDF or a photo from Files or Photos.
  • Photos: no permission is needed, as the picker only gives the photo you choose.
  • Background App Refresh: turned off, notifications and alarms are rescheduled only when you open the app; those already scheduled still arrive.
  • Actions from a notification: in LetMeRemind, Settings, Security, “Unlock to act from a notification” makes “Paid,” “Done,” “Checked,” and “Snooze” ask you to unlock the iPhone first. It is off by default.
