Running7277 · Privacy Policy

Last updated: 30 October 2026

What data the app handles

Running7277 is a workout tracker for running, walking, cycling and hiking. Everything it handles stays on your iPhone, in the app's local database:

  • Workouts: start and end time, distance, duration, speed, pace, estimated calories, elevation, and the GPS track of each session (coordinates, altitude and speed point by point). The start and end of a workout are also given a place name (see the Apple services section below).
  • Planned routes: GPX files you import, kept as routes to follow, with their track points and a place name for their starting point.
  • Profile: weight, gender and date of birth are required — they feed the calorie estimate and a theoretical maximum heart rate. Name, height, email and phone number are optional fields you may fill in or leave empty; the app never uses them to contact anyone.
  • Weight history, virtual coach settings, and personal records computed from your own workouts.
  • Backups you create yourself: encrypted exports protected by a secret phrase, plain exports, and anonymized exports (profile and place names removed, GPS coordinates rounded to roughly 110 meters; the workout titles and notes you typed, and the weight history, are currently kept as-is in anonymized exports).

There is no account, no sign-in, and the developer has no server: the app sends nothing to the developer. It has no advertising and no analytics, and it is not directed at children; whatever your age, nothing is collected by the developer.

How this data is obtained

  • GPS: while a workout (or its start countdown) is running, the app reads your position with the best accuracy the system offers, keeping points at least 3 meters apart. With your permission, recording continues in the background so a workout survives a locked screen — iOS shows the standard location indicator while this happens.
  • Your own input: profile fields, workout titles and notes, coach settings.
  • GPX import: route files you pick yourself from the Files app, iCloud Drive or any file provider.
  • Apple Watch: the watch app sends no sensor data. It only displays the live metrics of the workout running on the iPhone and can send three commands back: pause, resume, stop.

What the data is used for

Everything serves the app's visible features and nothing else: live stats during a workout, history and records, calorie estimates from your weight, the virtual coach and its spoken announcements, turn-by-turn voice guidance on an imported route, the Live Activity on the Lock Screen, the watch display, and Siri shortcuts. Spoken announcements are generated on the device by the system voice; the audio background mode exists solely so they can play while the screen is locked. There is no profiling, no advertising, and no sharing for any other purpose.

Who else sees it

No third party receives your data: the app embeds no third-party code and makes no network connections of its own. The following Apple services receive a copy of specific data as part of normal operation:

  • Apple Maps place lookup: to label a workout, the coordinates of its start and end points — and the first point of each imported GPX route — are sent to Apple to get a place name back. The request carries no account or identifier from the app. There is no setting to turn this off; it fails silently when offline.
  • Apple Maps tiles: map backgrounds are loaded from Apple when a map is on screen.
  • Your iPhone backup (iCloud or computer): the app's database and the backup files it writes are included in the device backup, under the protections Apple applies to it. The secret phrase for encrypted backups is not included — see below.
  • iCloud Drive / Files: only when you import a GPX file or share an export, through the picker or share sheet you control.
  • Apple Watch: live workout metrics (distance, duration, pace, speed, sport, coach status) are mirrored to your paired watch; the GPS track itself is never sent to the watch, and the Live Activity shows the same metrics without any position.

Siri suggestions are indexed on the device only: the names of your imported routes are exposed to the system so you can say, for example, "start the route Riverside on Running".

How long it is kept, and how to delete

Data stays on your iPhone until you delete it. You can delete any workout or imported route from the app (swipe on its row), and you can reset the backup secret phrase from the backup screen — after a reset, previously encrypted backups become permanently unreadable, as the app warns you. Export files live in the app's documents and in whatever location you shared them to; they are yours to delete. Deleting the app removes its database and documents from the device. The secret phrase is kept in this iPhone's keychain only, protected by Face ID or your passcode, and is never synced or included in backups: if it is lost or reset, encrypted backups cannot be opened again by anyone, the developer included.

Your permissions and how to withdraw them

  • Location: asked when the app first opens, as "While Using the App", combined with background recording during workouts. Withdraw it anytime in Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Running7277. Without it the app cannot record new workouts; your history, records and imported routes remain fully usable.
  • Face ID: used only to unlock the backup secret phrase. Withdraw it in Settings → Face ID & Passcode → Other Apps; the app then falls back to your device passcode.
  • Siri: shortcuts can be disabled in Settings → Apps → Running7277 → Siri.

The app asks for nothing else: no notifications, no microphone, no camera, no photo library, no contacts. In its distributed version it writes no logs.
