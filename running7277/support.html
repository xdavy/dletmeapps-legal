Frequently asked questions

Why does the app use my location in the background?

So a workout keeps recording with the screen locked or the phone in your pocket. Recording runs only during a workout (and its start countdown), and iOS shows the standard location indicator the whole time. Location permission is "While Using the App"; you can withdraw it in Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Running7277 — the app then still shows your history but cannot record new workouts.

What does the Apple Watch app do?

It mirrors the workout running on your iPhone — distance, duration, pace, coach status — and lets you pause, resume or stop it from your wrist. It sends no sensor data and stores nothing on the watch. The Lock Screen Live Activity shows the same metrics, without any position.

How do backups and the secret phrase work?

Encrypted backups are protected by a secret phrase that the app generates and keeps in this iPhone's keychain, behind Face ID or your passcode — it is never synced, never included in device backups, and never known to the developer. Keep a copy of it somewhere safe: if it is lost or reset, existing encrypted backups become permanently unreadable and nobody can recover them. Plain and anonymized exports open without a phrase.

My GPX file will not import

The app reads standard GPX files (tracks or routes) from Strava, Komoot, Visorando, Google Maps and similar tools, picked through the Files app or iCloud Drive. Files over 20 MB are refused. If a file is refused, try exporting it again from its source as plain GPX; if it still fails, send it along with your message if its content is not private.

Does the app send my data anywhere?

No. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising, and no developer server. The only data that leaves the device goes to Apple services: place-name lookup for workout start and end points, Apple Maps tiles, and your own iPhone backup. Details are in the privacy policy.
